# Legal Language Locks

Script language must preserve procedural and evidentiary status.

## Required distinctions
- **ALLEGATION** — asserted but not established.
- **COMPLAINT** — allegations pleaded by a plaintiff.
- **CLAIM** — an asserted legal/factual position; not automatically established.
- **CHARGE** — formal criminal accusation; not a conviction.
- **RULING** — a court decision on a defined issue; record exact scope.
- **VERDICT** — factfinder result; record claims/counts and later status.
- **SETTLEMENT** — negotiated resolution; never call it an admission unless the settlement/party expressly says so.
- **DISMISSAL** — claim/case ended at that stage; do not infer that all underlying allegations were proven false.
- **CONVICTION** — criminal guilt established by plea or verdict, subject to procedural status.
- **ADMISSION** — statement expressly admitted by the relevant party.
- **DENIAL** — disputed position, not proof of falsity.
- **UNDISPUTED_FACT** — supported and not materially disputed in the relevant record.
- **REPORTED_FACT** — responsibly reported but not itself a judicial finding.

## Wording examples
Complaint: “The lawsuit alleges…” / “According to the complaint…”
Charge: “Prosecutors charged X with…” not “X committed…”
Settlement: “The parties settled…” not “X admitted wrongdoing” unless sourced.
Ruling: say what the court actually held, not what a headline implies.

## Packaging lock
Titles/thumbnails may simplify, but may not make a materially stronger accusation than the evidence and current legal status support.
