# Visual Identity Lock — WHAT IT COST

Status: **LOCKED v1**

Locked on: 2026-09-20

This file is the canonical visual identity contract for the channel.

AI concept boards and channel mockups are references only. They do not override this file.

## 1. Identity principle

**Premium business documentary. Evidence, money, power, consequence.**

The channel must feel:
- cinematic;
- editorial;
- investigative;
- modern;
- controlled;
- expensive without looking like a luxury brand;
- serious without looking like a law firm.

The identity must not rely on courtroom clichés.

## 2. Primary wordmark

Public wordmark:
**WHAT IT COST**

Preferred construction:
- uppercase;
- heavy condensed display typography;
- stacked or compact horizontal treatment;
- strong black / paper-white contrast;
- one restrained signal-red mark or underline;
- no standalone acronym as the main identity.

### Primary avatar lock
Use a typography-first square composition:

`WHAT`
`IT COST`

or a tightly stacked three-line variant if readability requires it.

A short red bar may sit below or beside `IT` as the recurring brand signal.

The avatar must remain readable when rendered at 98 x 98 px.

### Forbidden primary marks
Do not use as the main logo:
- `WIC`;
- dollar-sign monograms;
- gavels;
- scales of justice;
- courthouse columns;
- handcuffs;
- stock candlestick arrows;
- generic legal seals.

## 3. Color system

### Core
- Deep Black — `#0B0B0B`
- Charcoal — `#1F1F1F`
- Slate — `#3A3A3A`
- Paper White — `#EDEDED`

### Signal
- Signal Red — `#D32222`

### Optional secondary accent
- Antique Gold — `#B69B68`

## Color rules
- Black / charcoal should dominate.
- Paper White carries primary text and document contrast.
- Signal Red is a semantic accent, not a background default.
- Antique Gold is optional and should remain rare; it must never compete with Signal Red.
- Do not introduce bright blue, green, purple or neon accents without an explicit visual-system revision.
- Avoid pure-white-on-pure-black everywhere; use tonal depth so the channel does not look like a generic template.

## 4. Typography system

### Display / thumbnails / major headings
**Bebas Neue** is the default production display reference.

Characteristics required if a substitute is used:
- condensed;
- bold;
- high-impact;
- excellent uppercase readability;
- narrow enough for 2–5 word thumbnail hooks.

### Supporting / UI / labels
**Inter**.

Use for:
- small captions;
- dates;
- source labels;
- lower thirds;
- diagrams;
- interface-style information;
- supporting copy.

### Editorial serif
**EB Garamond**, used sparingly.

Allowed for:
- one-line editorial quotes;
- document-style emphasis;
- occasional historical/editorial contrast.

Do not use serif typography as the dominant channel identity.

## Typography rules
- Headline hierarchy must remain readable on mobile.
- Never use more than two type families in one thumbnail.
- Do not stretch/compress fonts unnaturally.
- Do not commit font binaries to the repository; store only typography specifications and licensing/source notes.

## 5. Banner system

### Master
Recommended channel-banner master:
- 2560 x 1440 px;
- 16:9;
- JPG or PNG;
- <= 6 MB.

YouTube currently lists a minimum upload size of 2048 x 1152 and a text/logo safe area of 1235 x 338 at that minimum size.

For the 2560 x 1440 master, keep all essential brand text inside the proportionally scaled central safe zone (approximately 1544 x 423) and always verify the crop in YouTube Studio before publishing.

### Locked composition
The banner must contain:
- WHAT IT COST wordmark;
- tagline: **Where business decisions get expensive.**

Optional supporting visual language:
- original/copyright-cleared documentary collage;
- paper texture;
- redactions;
- abstract business architecture;
- evidence/document fragments;
- non-specific city/business imagery;
- original/generated classical/editorial motif.

### Banner exclusions
Do not use:
- random company-logo collage;
- fake court filings presented as real;
- real signatures without a sourced editorial reason;
- subscriber counts;
- view counts;
- fake awards;
- fake press quotes;
- identifiable third-party branding that implies sponsorship/ownership;
- decorative borders or frames that conflict with YouTube crop behavior.

## 6. Avatar export

Preferred master:
- 1024 x 1024 px;
- PNG;
- square source with central composition.

The primary public avatar remains the typography-first WHAT IT COST mark.

Before export, test at:
- 98 x 98;
- 48 x 48;
- small mobile comment/profile context.

If the words stop reading at small size, simplify spacing before introducing a different icon.

## 7. Thumbnail grammar

The channel should look related across videos without using a rigid template.

### Core composition
Usually use:
- 1 dominant subject;
- 1 secondary evidence/context element;
- 1 tension device;
- restrained red accent;
- strong negative space.

Possible dominant subjects:
- recognizable person;
- product;
- company mark used editorially;
- object tied to the dispute;
- building/location only when it is genuinely relevant.

Possible evidence/context:
- authentic filing/document crop;
- contract fragment;
- verified dollar figure;
- ownership diagram;
- product detail;
- timeline/date;
- source-backed headline fragment.

### Thumbnail text
Target:
- 0–4 words normally;
- 5 words only when exceptionally readable.

Thumbnail text should create a second layer with the title, not repeat it.

### Visual hierarchy
At phone size, the viewer should understand:
1. who/what;
2. conflict/stakes;
3. one unresolved question or consequence.

### Signal-red usage
Signal Red can mark:
- consequence;
- disputed point;
- highlighted amount;
- redaction;
- break/failure;
- directional tension.

Do not paint the entire thumbnail red.

### Face use
Faces are optional, not mandatory.

If used:
- one primary face is preferred;
- expression must come from a legitimate source or clearly illustrative reconstruction;
- do not manipulate a real person's expression to fabricate an event or reaction.

## 8. Thumbnail technical export

For long-form videos:
- master: 3840 x 2160 px;
- aspect ratio: 16:9;
- JPG or PNG;
- desktop upload file should remain within YouTube's current limit;
- maintain a clean high-resolution source even if a smaller derivative is exported.

A/B variants must be meaningfully different in concept, not tiny cosmetic changes.

## 9. Evidence and document treatment

On-screen documents belong to one of the provenance classes already defined by the production OS.

### Authentic source
May be shown as evidence when:
- source is recorded;
- crop/context is not misleading;
- legal/right-of-use review is satisfied.

### Editor recreation
Must preserve the verified text/meaning and must not be passed off as the original.

### Illustrative reconstruction
Must not appear to be an authentic filing/email/contract/signature/quote.

## Redaction language
Redaction bars are part of the brand grammar, but:
- do not fabricate a redaction in a way that implies an actual source was redacted;
- decorative redaction texture must be visibly editorial;
- never hide contradictory source text to create a false impression.

## 10. Channel-page visual rhythm

The first row of uploads should feel like one channel, not five templates.

Across a five-video row:
- vary subject placement;
- vary document/portrait/object balance;
- keep typography family consistent;
- keep black/paper/red palette consistent;
- avoid putting identical red bars in the exact same place every time;
- avoid identical three-line headline construction on every thumbnail.

Consistency comes from visual language, not copy-paste layout.

## 11. Showcase mockup rule

Channel mockups may include hypothetical videos and interface layout for internal planning.

They must be labeled or treated as **INTERNAL / CONCEPT**.

Do not publicly present mockups containing fabricated:
- subscriber counts;
- views;
- upload dates;
- comments;
- awards;
- press endorsements;
- sponsorships;
- verified badges;
- performance claims

as if they are real channel metrics.

Mockups are design simulations only.

## 12. Rights / platform guardrails

The visual identity must not:
- impersonate another creator/company/channel;
- intentionally copy another channel's profile picture, banner or distinctive branding;
- imply affiliation with a company merely because its logo appears in an editorial thumbnail;
- present generated/recreated material as authentic evidence;
- use misleading thumbnail imagery unrelated to the actual video;
- turn an allegation into a visual declaration of guilt.

Third-party logos/people/documents are episode-level editorial assets and still require rights/provenance review.

## 13. Do / Do Not

### DO
- lead with one clear visual idea;
- show real documents when they matter;
- make numbers visually concrete;
- use red sparingly;
- build tension through composition;
- preserve source context;
- test thumbnails at phone size;
- keep the channel premium and editorial.

### DO NOT
- use generic courtroom stock as default;
- cover every thumbnail with text;
- force a face into every topic;
- add random charts to look "business";
- fabricate screenshots or filings;
- use sensational legal words unsupported by case status;
- build thumbnails around decorative complexity;
- publish internal showcase metrics as real.
