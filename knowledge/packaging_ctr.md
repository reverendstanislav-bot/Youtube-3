# Packaging — titles & thumbnails that get clicked and keep trust

For packaging, hook-doctor, scout, critic. Channel grammar is in `channel/brand.md`; legal limits in `channel/legal.md` and `knowledge/legal_media_us.md`.

## 1. What a click needs
A viewer decides in ~1 second, mostly on the thumbnail, then confirms with the title. A winning package creates **a curiosity gap that feels safe to open**: the viewer knows exactly what world this is (clarity) and badly wants one missing piece (curiosity).
- **Clarity:** recognizable subject (company/product/person) readable at phone size.
- **Curiosity:** an unexpected number, contradiction, consequence or question.
- **Stakes:** money, power, a fall, a fight.
- Too much mystery = no click (viewer doesn't know what it is). Too much clarity = no click (viewer already knows the answer).

## 2. Titles
- 40–65 characters; the most important words first (mobile truncates the end).
- One idea. No colons stacking two titles.
- Concrete beats abstract: numbers, names, objects.
- Proven structures for this niche: "How X Lost $Y Because of Z" · "The [Tiny Thing] That Cost [Company] $Y" · "Why [Company] Is Suing [Company]" (only while fresh) · "[Person] Tried to [Goal]. It Cost Him [Z]." · "The [Company] Scandal Nobody Explained" · "Inside the $Y [Scheme/Deal/Mistake]".
- Title and thumbnail text must **complement**, never repeat: the title names the story, the thumbnail adds the emotional/visual hook (a number, a word, a face, an object).
- Every disputed claim is attributed inside the title ("Says", "Accused", "Lawsuit Claims") or avoided. Clicks from overstatement are paid back in retention loss and legal risk.
- Avoid ALL CAPS titles, emoji, and "(Explained)" filler.

## 3. Thumbnails
- **Three elements max:** one dominant subject, one supporting element, one tension device (arrow, red mark, contrast, gap between two objects).
- **0–4 words** of text, huge, high contrast; the text must be readable at 150 px wide.
- Faces with a clear emotion raise CTR — but only real, sourced photos of real people, never generated or altered expressions. Objects, documents, money, logos and numbers work well for this channel without faces.
- One focal point; the eye should land on it first. Negative space is a feature.
- Channel consistency through palette (black, paper white, Signal Red) and type, not through identical layouts.
- Test at phone size next to competitors' thumbnails from `yt_search.md`: ours must stand out, not blend in.

## 4. Differentiation from competitors
- Look at the top videos on the same topic: which words, faces, colors, numbers do they use? Do something visibly different (if all show a logo on red, use a document or a person on black).
- If competitors own "breaking news", own "the whole story" / "the hidden mechanism" / "what it cost".
- A later video can still win with a clearly deeper promise ("The Documents Behind…", "The Full Story of…") — but only if the video actually is deeper.

## 5. A/B testing
- YouTube Test & Compare allows up to 3 thumbnails (and titles). Prepare 2–3 genuinely different concepts (different subject or tension device), not color variants.
- Judge by watch time share, not CTR alone.

## 6. Promise → first 30 seconds
Write down the exact promise the package makes. The first 30 s of the script must confirm it with a concrete fact. If the video cannot deliver the promise by the end, change the package, not the facts.

## 7. Shorts packaging
- Title = the hook sentence, 40–60 chars, no clickbait.
- First frame = the thumbnail; strong text on frame in the top two thirds.
- Description line links the full video.
