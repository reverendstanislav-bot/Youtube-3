# Source Capture Guide

## Goal
Make every material claim reconstructable later without relying on chat memory.

## For each source
Create a `SOURCE_INDEX.csv` row immediately when the source becomes material.

Capture:
- stable source_id;
- exact title/document name;
- publisher/court/agency;
- author when relevant;
- URL;
- docket/case identifiers when applicable;
- filing/publication date;
- access date;
- primary vs secondary;
- authority level;
- archive/local reference when permitted;
- status and notes.

## Court / legal materials
Prefer the underlying filing/order/judgment/verdict/agency record over summaries when the precise procedural status matters.

When a source is long, record in notes:
- page/paragraph/section;
- what proposition it supports;
- whether the language is allegation, evidence, finding or ruling.

## Company / party statements
A company statement proves the company said something. It does not automatically prove the underlying assertion.

## Journalism
Use high-quality journalism for context, reporting, interviews and discovery. Material disputed claims should be corroborated where practical.

## Screenshots / documents used visually
The asset record should link back to the source_id and note crop/context/rights provenance.

## Stale or superseded source
Do not delete history. Mark SUPERSEDED and add the newer source/event.
