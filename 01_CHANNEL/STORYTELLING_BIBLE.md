# Storytelling Bible

## Default story engine
1. Familiar surface — recognizable company/product/creator.
2. Hidden problem.
3. Stakes — money, ownership, market power, reputation or survival.
4. Conflict — sides with incompatible goals.
5. Mechanism — contract, lawsuit, IP, ownership, trade secret, fraud theory, arbitration or regulation.
6. Escalation — filings, discoveries, strategic mistakes, reversals.
7. Reveal — clause/document/action that reframes the story.
8. Consequence.

## Opening
No generic history lesson. Start with the contradiction, extraordinary claim, money figure, recognizable object/company or decisive consequence. Clarify procedural status early enough that suspense never relies on a false statement.

## Narrative order vs chronology
Chronology is canonical in `CASE_EVENT_LEDGER.csv`. The edit may reorder reveals but may not create false causality.

## Legal exposition
Explain only what unlocks the story. Return quickly to people, money, decisions and consequences.

## Ending
Prefer concrete consequences and unresolved implications. If a matter remains active, say so clearly.
