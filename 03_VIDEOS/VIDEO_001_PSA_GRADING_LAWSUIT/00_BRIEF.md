# VIDEO 001 — Brief

## Working title
**The PSA Lawsuit That Could Change Card Grading**

## Subject / entities
- Professional Sports Authenticator (PSA) / Collectors Universe, Inc.
- Collectors Holdings, Inc.
- Plaintiff Nicholas Funk
- Broader Collectors ecosystem where relevant to the pleaded conflict

## Pillar
BUSINESS_LAWSUIT

## Core viewer promise
Explain why a newly filed federal lawsuit attacks not just one card grade, but the business system that turns a grading number into market value — and what would actually have to happen in court before the headline allegations mean anything.

## Hidden problem / mechanism
The complaint alleges that grading marketed as reliable and independent is shaped by undisclosed subjectivity, incentives and conflicts across grading-related businesses. The legal mechanism is a proposed civil RICO/class-action theory plus consumer/common-law claims.

## Money / stakes
The complaint is reported as alleging roughly $1 billion in grading fees during the relevant period. Civil RICO can provide threefold damages for proven qualifying injury under 18 U.S.C. § 1964(c).

**Guardrail:** "$3 billion" is a theoretical treble-damages framing, not an award, judgment, settlement, certified damages model or guaranteed recovery.

## Conflict
Collectors/PSA's grading credibility and business model vs. a plaintiff alleging systemic deception and financially conflicted grading practices.

## Consequence
Potential consequences include litigation-driven disclosure, changes to grading practices, damages or injunctive relief if claims survive and are proven. At this stage those outcomes are unresolved.

## Why now
Funk v. Collectors Universe, Inc. et al., No. 1:26-cv-02933, was filed July 28, 2026. Current September 2026 coverage still describes the case as pending and the allegations as unproven.
