# VIDEO 001 — 01 Evidence Research

Status: **IN_PROGRESS**

## Research question
What does Funk v. Collectors actually allege about PSA's grading business, what evidence supports or merely illustrates those allegations, and what would have to happen for the case to change card grading in practice?

## Current verified anchors
1. Nicholas Funk filed a federal action against Collectors Universe, Inc. d/b/a PSA and Collectors Holdings, Inc. on July 28, 2026 in the U.S. District Court for the District of Maryland, No. 1:26-cv-02933.
2. The docket identifies the nature of suit as Racketeer/Corrupt Organization and the cause as 18 U.S.C. § 1964.
3. Public reporting describes the filing as a 188-page proposed class action asserting civil RICO and other claims. These are allegations, not findings.
4. 18 U.S.C. § 1964(c) provides threefold damages for qualifying injury proven by a successful civil RICO plaintiff.
5. PSA's official backlog tracker reported 9.9 million active units on September 8, 2026, showing the company is operating at very large current scale.
6. Recent September 14 industry coverage still described Funk and two other 2026 Collectors/PSA cases as unresolved.

## Primary-source acquisition queue
Priority A:
- Filed Funk complaint with stable copy and page/paragraph mapping.
- Current D. Maryland docket through research date.
- Any summons/service/appearance/extension/response filings.
- PSA/Collectors public or filed response specific to Funk.

Priority B:
- PSA current grading standards.
- PSA terms/arbitration/class waiver relevant to direct vs intermediary submitters.
- PSA historical/current marketing statements used by the complaint.
- Collectors corporate materials describing ownership/business relationships.
- Official records for historical examples the complaint relies on.

## Do not merge cases
Keep Funk separate from:
- Rasmussen v. Collectors Holdings, Inc., C.D. Cal. No. 8:26-cv-00897 (antitrust/acquisitions).
- Lichtman v. Collectors Universe, Inc., C.D. Cal. No. 8:26-cv-02048 (individual grading/conflict allegations).
- Curcio litigation or criminal matter.

Related cases may provide context only after their own claims/events are sourced.

## Key verification tracks

### A. "$3B" mechanism
Determine:
- exact complaint language for alleged grading fees/loss;
- whether the complaint pleads a classwide damages number or uses revenue/fees as a proxy;
- which counts could support treble damages;
- whether "$3B" is merely arithmetic extrapolation.

### B. Grading representation
Compare the complaint's characterization against PSA's own current/historical public wording about:
- objectivity;
- expert graders;
- consistency;
- eye appeal/judgment;
- altered cards;
- reviews/crossovers/regrades.

### C. Business incentives / vertical integration
Map which Collectors businesses actually:
- grade;
- price;
- vault;
- buy/sell;
- lend/finance;
- provide liquidity.

Then distinguish corporate ownership facts from plaintiff's inference about conflicts.

### D. Historical card examples
For each dramatic example:
- source the underlying card/certification;
- source any admitted alteration/fraud;
- establish what PSA knew and when, if evidence exists;
- separate later-known facts from knowledge allegedly held at grading time.

### E. Procedure
Track:
- service;
- appearances;
- amended complaint;
- motions to dismiss/transfer/compel arbitration;
- class certification;
- discovery;
- rulings.

The ending of the video depends on current posture.
