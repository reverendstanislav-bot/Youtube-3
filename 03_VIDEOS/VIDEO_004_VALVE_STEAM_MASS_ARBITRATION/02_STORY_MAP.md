## Viewer promise

Show how covered individual arbitration met thousands of filings, how Valve changed the agreement and sued claimants, and why the unresolved contract question produced possible immediate appellate review. Separate documented actions and Valve-attributed reasons from inferred motives.

## Chronological truth

1. Valve added mandatory individual arbitration and a class-action waiver to the Steam Subscriber Agreement in 2012.
2. Consumer antitrust claims were directed to arbitration while publisher litigation proceeded separately in court.
3. The May2026 district order recorded4,991 claimants pursuing AAA arbitration; this is a later recorded scale, not an exactSeptember2024/current count.
4. In 2024, enforceability disputes developed inside some arbitrations.
5. On September 26, 2024, Valve removed arbitration and the class waiver and added court-only language covering older claims.
6. Valve requested administrative closure onSeptember27,2024; AAA responded onOctober7, declining closure without deciding the contract merits.
7. Valve filed in federal court to stop them.
8. The procedural path produced an August 2025 complaint against 572 defendants and a request for preliminary relief.
9. The district court denied preliminary relief on May 27, 2026.
10. The court certified the contract question for interlocutory appeal and stayed the case on July 30, 2026.
11. The reviewed public mirror records appeal26-6269 opening September25,2026. The snapshot throughSeptember28 contains no merits ruling; official current/release status remains unchecked.

## Narrative order

### Cold open — the reversal

Steam once told users that disputes had to be handled one at a time. Then thousands used that route, Valve removed arbitration from its own agreement, and asked a court to stop cases already underway. End the hook with the unresolved question: can a company rewrite the forum after thousands of disputes have begun?

### Act I — why the clause existed

Explain individual arbitration, the class waiver and the apparent business logic: prevent aggregation, move disputes outside court, and resolve them claimant by claimant. Avoid doctrine-heavy exposition.

### Act II — individual claims become mass pressure

Reveal that “individual” did not necessarily mean “small.” Coordinated filing produced thousands of separate matters and multiplied administrative steps and possible fees. Introduce 4,991 only with the court-record attribution.

### Act III — Valve changes the agreement

Show the September 2024 announcement and compare the old and new text. The new agreement removes arbitration and the class waiver, sends disputes to King County courts and expressly reaches earlier claims. This is the visual and conceptual midpoint.

### Act IV — the old cases do not simply disappear

AAA declines to close the pending matters administratively. Arbitrators and parties disagree over which agreement controls. Results are mixed. Valve turns to federal court.

### Act V — hundreds of users become defendants

Move from 624 respondents in the first filing to 572 defendants in the operative complaint, then to Valve's estimate that roughly 400 remained active by oral argument. Explain that movement can reflect completed, withdrawn or otherwise resolved matters—not one global settlement.

### Act VI — preliminary ruling and appeal

The district court finds Valve has not met the preliminary-injunction burden and treats the retroactive clause as likely unenforceable against these defendants at that stage. Then add the essential counterweight: the judge says the issue is novel, reasonable jurists can disagree, certifies immediate appeal and stays the case.

### Ending — the clause is now the case

The antitrust allegations are still unresolved. The immediate appellate fight is about which contract controls already pending arbitrations. End on the business lesson: drafting a dispute system around individual proceedings does not eliminate collective pressure; it can change the form that pressure takes.

## Core characters and entities

- **Valve / Steam:** contract drafter, platform operator, arbitration participant and federal plaintiff.
- **Steam users / defendants:** individual arbitration claimants with related antitrust allegations; not a single undifferentiated bloc.
- **AAA:** administrator, not the judge of the federal contract dispute.
- **Federal district court:** denied preliminary relief and certified the question.
- **Ninth Circuit:** current unresolved appellate forum.
- **Claimant firms:** supporting context only; keep separate *Bucher Law* litigation clearly labeled.

## Money and incentives

- Valve's stated contract position: stop proceedings it says are no longer authorized. Potential multiplied defense/administration cost is structural analysis, not a proven motive for removing arbitration.
- Claimants' incentive: preserve the forum and procedural rights under which their cases were filed.
- AAA schedule: costs scale through initiation, per-case, appointment, final and arbitrator-compensation stages.
- Prohibited shortcut: no unverified `$20 million` hook.

## Hidden mechanism

A class waiver can prevent one aggregated case while still permitting thousands of individual cases. When coordinated claims arrive at scale, per-case procedures and fees create aggregate pressure through a system designed around individual treatment.

## Escalation beats

1. One contract clause.
2. Thousands of separate filings.
3. A rewritten contract.
4. AAA refuses administrative closure.
5. Hundreds of users named in federal court.
6. Preliminary injunction denied.
7. Judge certifies the issue because the answer is genuinely contestable.
8. Appeal becomes the unresolved final beat.

## Reversals and counterarguments

- “Valve banned class actions, so users filed individually.” True as structure, but do not claim the filings were automatically valid.
- “Valve lost.” Too broad. Valve lost the preliminary-injunction motion; appeal is active and underlying antitrust merits remain open.
- “Every arbitrator sided with users.” False; the record describes mixed outcomes.
- “Valve owed $20 million.” Unverified and excluded.
- “The 2024 agreement obviously controls.” Disputed; that is the appellate issue.

## Legal exposition needed

- Difference between a preliminary injunction and final judgment.
- Difference between individual arbitration and a class action.
- Meaning of a retroactive forum-selection clause in plain language.
- Purpose of interlocutory appeal: appellate review before final judgment on a controlling legal question.
