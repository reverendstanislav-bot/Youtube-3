## Locked thesis

Valve required many Steam users to resolve disputes individually through AAA arbitration and barred class proceedings. Thousands later used that route. Valve then removed mandatory arbitration and the class waiver, added court-only language reaching older claims, and asked a federal court to halt pending arbitrations. The court denied preliminary relief, certified the contract question for interlocutory appeal, and stayed the case. This is a contract-design and mass-filing story, not proof that Valve lost the underlying antitrust claims.

## Contract mechanism

The April 25, 2023 SSA required individual AAA arbitration and barred class, representative and combined arbitration. The September 2024 SSA moved disputes to court in King County, removed the class waiver, and said the court clause included claims arising before the new or prior agreement.

Valve's evidence, summarized by the court, said 454 of 572 defendants affirmatively accepted and 118 later logged in without affirmative acceptance. Defendants argued the choice was one-sided because account deletion affected game libraries. Their claimed average library value above $3,000 is disputed by Valve and must not be presented as fact.

## Scale and money guardrail

Supported figures: 4,991 AAA claimants at the cited stage; 624 original respondents; 572 complaint defendants; roughly 400 active by May 2026 according to Valve; 224 stays according to Valve; eleven original-SSA rulings according to defendants' evidence.

AAA's January 2024 schedule used an $8,125 business initiation fee, tiered per-case charges, appointment fees, a $600 business final fee per case and separate arbitrator compensation. These mechanics explain aggregate cost pressure but do **not** establish a single $20 million Valve invoice, liability or payment. Do not use `$20 million` without authentic invoices and payment records.
