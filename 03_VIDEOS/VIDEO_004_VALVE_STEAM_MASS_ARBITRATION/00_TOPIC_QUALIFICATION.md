## Strongest hook

Valve required Steam users to resolve disputes one at a time in arbitration and barred class proceedings. When large numbers of users pursued that individual route, Valve changed the Steam Subscriber Agreement to send disputes to court and sought to halt already pending arbitrations. The legally safe story is about the cost and strategic reversal created by dispute-resolution design—not a claim that Valve lost the underlying antitrust case.

## Current procedural snapshot

- In October 2024 Valve filed to enjoin arbitrations brought by hundreds of Steam users.
- Valve later filed a civil complaint naming hundreds of defendants.
- On May 27, 2026, the Western District of Washington denied Valve's motion for a preliminary injunction. The ruling concerns preliminary relief and likelihood of success; it is not a final merits judgment on the consumers' antitrust allegations.
- On July 30, 2026, the district court certified the preliminary-injunction order for interlocutory appeal and stayed that case pending the Ninth Circuit's decision whether to take the appeal.
- Separate consumer, publisher, law-firm and individual-award proceedings exist. They must not be merged into one procedural storyline.

## Competition gap

Direct YouTube treatments already exist and commonly package the story around a claimed $20 million fee dispute or portray mass arbitration as an exploit. The channel's defensible gap is a document-led chronology showing how the original arbitration/class-waiver design, mass filing economics, the 2024 contract revision, the injunction denial and the appeal fit together—while keeping the underlying antitrust allegations and separate cases distinct.

## Money guardrail

Do not state that Valve definitively owed, refused to pay, lost, or was ordered to pay a single global "$20 million" amount unless Stage 01 traces the exact figure, claimant set, administrator invoice, payment status and procedural record. Arbitration administration fees are not damages, a settlement, or an antitrust judgment.

## Legal / reputational guardrails

1. The consumers' antitrust assertions remain allegations unless tied to a specific award or ruling.
2. The May 2026 order denied preliminary injunctive relief; it did not finally adjudicate the antitrust claims.
3. The court's unconscionability analysis was made in the preliminary-injunction posture and the order was certified for interlocutory appeal.
4. Do not describe every claimant, arbitration, law firm or award as part of one unified case.
5. Keep `Valve v. Abbruzzese`, `In re Valve Antitrust Litigation`, individual award-vacatur cases and `Valve v. Bucher Law` separate.
6. Do not call claimant recruitment, mass arbitration or Valve's litigation strategy extortion, fraud or abuse as fact. Attribute such accusations to the party that made them.
7. Do not imply the 2024 Steam agreement retroactively ended pending arbitrations; that is a disputed legal issue.
8. Refresh all dockets at Stage 01 and again at Stage 16.
