## 1. What the movie is REALLY about
Three separable arcs must be connected but never fused into one false legal outcome:

**A — Google / Waymo confidential-file episode, 2015–17.** Engineer Anthony Levandowski worked for Google's Chauffeur autonomous project, later known as Waymo. DOJ's March 19, 2020 account of his plea details the Dec 11, 2015 download of ~14,000 Google internal SVN files and transfer to a personal computer on or about Dec 14. He also copied other Google Drive materials. A *specific* Google Drive project-status document, NOT every one of the ~14,000 SVN files, was the subject of his one-count federal plea. [C001–C005, S010–S011]

**B — Uber's Otto acquisition and corporate civil fight, 2016–18.** Levandowski founded Otto companies shortly before/after leaving Google; Uber and Otto entered a Feb 2016 term sheet and completed a transaction in Aug 2016. Waymo sued Uber and the startup in Feb 2017, alleging trade-secret misappropriation involving LiDAR (laser sensing) and other claims. Judge Alsup's May 2017 *provisional* order found compelling evidence concerning the downloads and serious questions surrounding Uber, partly enjoining conduct and limiting Levandowski's LiDAR work. Uber contested receipt/use of Waymo secrets. The civil jury trial started in February 2018, but the case ended in a Feb 9 stipulation to dismiss **with prejudice** following a **confidential settlement**. There was **no jury verdict** establishing all alleged Uber misconduct. [C006–C020,C036,C039,C040; S001–S006,S009]

**C — Individual criminal disposition and corporate afterlife, 2019–26.** A federal grand jury indicted Levandowski in 2019 on 33 counts. He pleaded guilty to **one** count and was sentenced Aug 2020 to **18 months**, a **$95,000 fine** and **$756,499.22 restitution**. The DOJ pardon register shows his presidential pardon on Jan 19, 2021. Uber separately announced Dec 2020 that it would divest Advanced Technologies Group to Aurora, then completed the sale Jan 19, 2021. Later Waymo and Uber became operating partners: Phoenix Oct 2023, Austin Mar 2025, Atlanta Jun 2025. At Oct 13, 2026 Waymo still advertises Austin/Atlanta service via Uber. This surprising epilogue is **chronological contrast**, NOT proof that the litigation was fabricated, legally reversed, or the reason for the partnership. [C024–C035,C038; S010–S021]

**Critical distinction:** The civil **Waymo v Uber** case, Levandowski's **federal criminal** case, and Delaware **McElrath v Kalanick** Uber shareholder-derivative case are three separate proceedings and resolve different questions. Never use a finding in one as automatic proof of a claim in another.

## 5. Each side — what can be said
**Waymo allegations (S001):** Levandowski copied extensive confidential design files, including LiDAR-related material, and Uber/Otto benefited. Repeated count/claims were not all tried to verdict.

**Judge Alsup's 2017 preliminary findings (S003):** strong evidence of the personal downloads; court reasoned Uber likely knew or should have known he retained confidential files and noted serious questions of potential seepage. This is **a narrower interim finding**, sufficient for specified limited relief. Do NOT replace it with absolute statement "court confirmed Uber stole 14,000 trade secrets."

**Uber defense/response (S009):** CEO Dara Khosrowshahi explicitly denied Uber incorporated Waymo trade secrets or proprietary code into its AV technology while conceding Otto acquisition handling raised concerns. The reported public denial must appear near the related allegation, not be omitted from the documentary.

**After settlement (S013):** Uber S-1 describes an independent software expert appointed under settlement and interim questions about some software functions. Do not claim a final infringement conclusion from interim issues; do not claim exoneration simply because Uber denied use.

**Separate Delaware corporate governance case (S007–S008):** shareholder derivative claims were dismissed on **demand requirement**, not on a finding that the Waymo case was fictional. This record is valuable for deal structure and board approval, not as a new Uber theft finding.

**Levandowski criminal case (S010–S012):** specific Google Drive tracking document recognized as trade secret; plea one count; original 33-count indictment is not 33 convictions. Pardon is clemency, not a new determination that the conduct never happened.
