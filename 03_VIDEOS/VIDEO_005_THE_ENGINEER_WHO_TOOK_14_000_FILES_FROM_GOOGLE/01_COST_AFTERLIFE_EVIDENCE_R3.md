## Finding 1: Missing evidence was a dramatic judicial problem before settlement
**Primary**: Judge Alsup, Jan 30 2018 Dkt 2585, S031, https://docs.justia.com/cases/federal/district-courts/california/candce/3:2017cv00939/308136/2585.
Judge addressed five computer discs Levandowski had supposedly destroyed, Uber's delayed disclosure of the discs' fate and deletion of communications. Court decided jurors **may, but need not, draw adverse inference** from delayed disclosure. Jury could alternatively believe Uber sought to avoid misuse, not conceal it. **Unknown contents / non-compulsory inference / no verdict** are mandatory qualifiers. This is NOT judicial proof that Uber received all of the 14k files or that actual destruction was personally watched by anyone.

## Finding 2: The engineer had a second financial legal case, not captured in R2
**Primary**: In re Levandowski, bankruptcy court, Jan24 2025 opinion S032 https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-canb-3_20-bk-30242/pdf/USCOURTS-canb-3_20-bk-30242-0.pdf, originally docket 20-30242 Doc#1318.
The **2019 Google arbitration** concerned separate alleged breaches of employment/confidentiality/recruiting obligations and resulted in a corrected award against Levandowski. **March 4, 2020** California superior court confirmed **$179,047,998.64** in civil judgment against **Levandowski individually**. **Hours later** he petitioned for Chapter 11 protection. Neither the amount nor the arbitration is the 2020 individual federal criminal plea/sentence, nor the 2018 Waymo/Uber $245m equity corporate settlement. See C059–C061,C068, E042–E044.

## Finding 3: Uber's own acquisition document created a dispute over whether it would pay
**Primary**: bankruptcy judge S032 and Uber SEC Q2 2022 S034 https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1543151/000154315122000024/uber-20220630.htm.
April 11 2016 Uber made an **indemnification agreement** with Levandowski and certain colleagues. Levandowski later claimed Uber owed indemnification of the Google judgment; Uber disputed whether obligations applied under conditions/exclusions. It was a legally disputed allocation of risks, **not a blanket unconditional insurance promise**. Litigation and Chapter11 filings followed. See C062,C069, E045.

## Finding 4: Additional Uber payment was ACTUALLY made in 2022 — exact amount sealed
**Primary**: federal district court S033 https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-cand-4_22-cv-02781/pdf/USCOURTS-cand-4_22-cv-02781-0.pdf, March14 2023 Dkt77, especially page3/4; bankruptcy opinion S032.
A 2022 three-way global settlement between **Uber, Google, Levandowski** resolved outstanding claims between them. The deal included an **'indefeasible' direct Uber Main Payment to Google** whose amount was under seal, and an additional **$2,000,000** for Levandowski's bankruptcy estate to fund the plan. The federal judge expressly reported that Uber actually made Main Payment **on or before May 20, 2022**. **This is not 2018 $245m Waymo equity, not known to equal $179m judgment, and $2m is separate from secret total.** Sources S032/S033/S034, C063–C065,C067, E046–E048.

## Finding 5: 2025 tax continuation does NOT undo 2022 global settlement
**Primary**: bankruptcy court S032 p3, 2025-01-24. On remand judge treated direct Uber-to-Google transfer as **gross income to Levandowski** for tax purposes, but did **not** assess final tax liabilities or question the agreement's enforceability. This is separate legal aftermath, not another Uber financial cash award. See C066,E049.
