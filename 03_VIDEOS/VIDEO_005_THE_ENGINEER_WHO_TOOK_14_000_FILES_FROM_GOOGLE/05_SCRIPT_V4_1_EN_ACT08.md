# VIDEO005 English V4.1 Act A08 — The contract and sealed payment

**Continuation of the SAME long-form narration, after A07-P06; not an additional video.**

**A08-P01**

Remember the agreement signed in connection with Uber's purchase of Otto? It addressed certain legal liabilities for employees making the move. In 2020, Levandowski turned to Uber, arguing that the agreement required the company to cover Google's judgment against him. Uber disputed the agreement's scope and application. The old deal had become the center of a new lawsuit.

**A08-P02**

Here's the mechanism hidden behind the acquisition headline. A buyer can agree on a price for a business and separately negotiate who might bear certain legal risks. Those promises are not the same thing. One looks forward to what the company might become. The other can end up in court years after the transaction—and long after the deal announcement is forgotten.

**A08-P03**

It would be wrong to say Uber automatically owed every dollar Levandowski faced. The indemnification terms were conditional, and the parties fought about their enforceability. But the public record gives us something firmer than a guess about anyone's intentions. In 2022, Google, Uber, and Levandowski reached a separate global settlement connected to his bankruptcy.

**A08-P04**

In 2022, Uber paid Google directly under a separate settlement involving Anthony Levandowski. Court records confirm the main payment was made by May 20. Its amount remains sealed. The agreement also provided another $2 million to fund Levandowski's bankruptcy plan. Those are separate payments, and neither number tells us the confidential amount.

**A08-P05**

Here's the answer to the question we started with. Uber didn't pay Google again because some secret Waymo jury verdict had appeared. Levandowski argued a 2016 indemnification agreement required Uber to cover his personal judgment. Uber disputed that. The parties reached a new settlement in 2022—and Uber paid Google under that settlement. The court did not treat the payment as performance of the old contract.

**A08-P06**

So how much did Uber actually pay Google? That's the one number the public record won't give us. Court filings establish that the direct payment happened. They do not reveal the principal amount. It would be wrong to substitute the $179 million personal judgment. It would also be wrong to infer it from the separate $2 million used to fund the bankruptcy plan.
