## Story and differentiator
A Google self-driving project engineer copied about 14,000 Google SVN files in December 2015, left the company, formed a startup later acquired by Uber. Waymo then sued Uber over alleged trade-secret misappropriation. The **civil case settled**, in Uber equity valued around **$245M at the time**, with **no jury verdict finding that Uber used all those files**. In a separate criminal case, Anthony Levandowski pleaded guilty to **one count concerning a specified confidential trade secret**, received an 18-month sentence, and was later pardoned. The twist: years after the dispute, Waymo and Uber publicly partnered to offer robotaxis through Uber (Austin and Atlanta).

**First-minute hook hypothesis (not locked VO):**
"An engineer downloaded around 14,000 files from Google's self-driving project. The startup he founded was bought by Uber. Waymo accused Uber of benefiting from stolen technology, and the case ended in a settlement valued around $245 million. Years later, those former courtroom enemies became robotaxi partners. But what did the records actually prove?"

## Legal and story hard limits
1. 14,000 downloaded/copied files **do not establish 14,000 guilty counts** or Uber deployment of all files.
2. Distinguish the individual's **criminal ADMISSION/CONVICTION** from Waymo's **civil ALLEGATIONS against Uber**, ultimately **SETTLED**; settlement is not criminal guilt or adverse jury verdict.
3. ~$245M = estimated equity settlement value of **0.34% of Uber**, not cash payment, confirmed theft loss, or damages awarded by a judge.
4. An estimated ~$680M startup acquisition cited in a court order is not verified as all-cash and must be checked before use.
5. Pardon does not mean a court exonerated Uber. The later commercial partnership is not proof that past allegations were false or true.
6. Original court exhibits and footage rights must be evaluated before visuals.
