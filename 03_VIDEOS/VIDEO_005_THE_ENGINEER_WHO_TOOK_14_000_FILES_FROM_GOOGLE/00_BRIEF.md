## The real story, revised
The story isn't merely a 2015 theft allegation followed by a 2018 stock settlement and a tidy Waymo–Uber partnership.
- A LiDAR vendor mistakenly sent Google engineering drawings meant for Uber **in Dec 2016**; the original court record ties this to suspicions about reuse. The source email must not be synthetically depicted as authentic. [C041,E034,S003,S008]
- Uber had drawn up a talent/acquisition opportunity, hired Stroz to investigate retained Google files, and shareholder litigation later examined allegations about counsel warnings, board briefings and risk allocation. Stroz's final report reportedly **found no evidence of the retained data transferred to Otto or other third parties**. Both halves belong together. [C042,C043,C046,C056,S007,S008]
- The deal was **headline $680m with conditional stock milestones and $100k upfront cash**, not $680m cash paid to one engineer. [C009–C011,S007]
- 2017 court ordered **limited preliminary relief**, but criticized parts of Waymo's patent theory and some of the 121 listed secrets. Uber maintained no Waymo trade secrets were used in its own systems. [C014–C018,C045,S003,S009]
- 2018 company suit settled through **Uber equity worth roughly $245m at the time**, with no final corporate jury verdict. 2020 Levandowski **one-count** conviction and 2021 pardon were individual, separate. [C020–C028,S005,S008,S010–S012]
- In 2026 both companies confirmed their **Phoenix pilot ended**; July reporting suggested possible broader conflict but did not establish a completed split in Austin/Atlanta. [C048–C050,C055,S021,S024–S026]
- In 2026 Kalanick publicly announced **Atoms raised $1.7b**. TechCrunch reported **Atoms acquired Levandowski's Pronto** and that **Uber invested ~$100m into Atoms**; not a direct Uber purchase of Pronto or proved 2026 robotaxi licensing deal. [C051–C054,S027–S029]
