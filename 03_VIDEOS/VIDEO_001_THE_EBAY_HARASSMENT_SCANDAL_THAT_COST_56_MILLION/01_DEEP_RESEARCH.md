## Research conclusion

The evidence base is sufficient to advance to Stage 02 without inventing facts.

The story is stronger than the original pitch because the record supports three distinct layers:

1. **Established campaign conduct** — eBay's own DPA admissions, guilty pleas, sentencing records and the 2025 federal court order establish the harassment, surveillance and obstruction core.
2. **Executive / corporate mechanism** — the summary-judgment record documents escalating internal communications and explains which theories against Wenig, Wymer, Jones and eBay were strong enough to reach a jury, while also preserving important limits.
3. **Measurable consequence** — eBay's 2024 DPA cost $3M plus a three-year monitor; the 2026 civil settlement package was announced at $55.7M, with $48.7M compensation and $7M charitable commitments.

The correct WHAT IT COST story is therefore not "eBay employees mailed cockroaches." It is:

**How executive hostility moved into a security operation, how the operation crossed into criminal conduct and obstruction, and how years of criminal and civil proceedings converted that failure into corporate, personal and financial consequences.**

## Parties / entities and roles

### Ina and David Steiner
Founders/publishers of EcommerceBytes, an e-commerce trade publication started in 1999.

### eBay Inc.
Public e-commerce company. Entered a three-year DPA in January 2024, accepted responsibility for former-employee misconduct, admitted a detailed factual recitation and agreed to a $3M criminal penalty and independent compliance monitor.

### Jim Baugh
Former Senior Director of Safety & Security and head of eBay's Security Department. Pleaded guilty and received 57 months imprisonment.

### Security-team participants
David Harville, Brian Gilbert, Stephanie Popp, Stephanie Stockwell, Philip Cooke and Veronica Zea. Together with Baugh, all seven criminally prosecuted participants pleaded guilty.

### Devin Wenig
Former eBay CEO. Not criminally charged. Civil claims against him survived significant portions of summary judgment. The 2025 order explicitly notes he did not communicate directly with Baugh about the Steiners; the relevant messages ran through Wymer. Final settlement contribution announced as $2M compensation plus $1M First Amendment charity contribution.

### Steve Wymer
Former Chief Communications Officer. Not criminally charged. The 2025 court order describes extensive communications with Baugh and held a reasonable jury could find he induced, encouraged or authorized tortious conduct. Final announced contribution: $50K compensation.

### Wendy Jones
Former SVP Global Operations and Baugh's direct supervisor. Not criminally charged. The 2025 order held several theories against her could proceed to a jury. Final announced contribution: $500K compensation.

### Progressive F.O.R.C.E. Concepts (PFC)
Security/intelligence staffing contractor that employed Zea. The court granted PFC summary judgment on the claims addressed, finding the record did not support knowing participation and that eBay exercised the relevant operational control over Zea.

## Canonical chronology

### Phase 1 — criticism becomes an executive issue
By spring 2019, internal communications show hostility toward EcommerceBytes and Ina Steiner. The court's summary-judgment record includes Wymer's April 10 "crush this lady" text, Wenig's May 31 "take her down" message and the August 1 message that "now is the time."

Key limitation:
These messages are highly relevant, but they are not themselves criminal convictions of Wenig/Wymer/Jones. Wenig, Wymer and Jones were not criminally charged.

### Phase 2 — executive concern moves into security
Jones and Baugh discussed the issue in May. The court record says Jones told Baugh to deal with it "off the radar" and not give her details. After a first Boston trip and fence graffiti, Baugh described a "tap on the shoulder" to Wymer.

On August 1, after Wenig's message, Wymer sent Baugh escalating language including "DONE", "BURNED DOWN", "ashes" and "Whatever it takes."

On August 6, Baugh held operation-planning meetings. On August 7, Baugh met with Wymer immediately before the campaign escalated.

### Phase 3 — the operation
The primary record establishes:
- vulgar/threatening Twitter messages;
- disturbing deliveries;
- live insects;
- bloody pig mask;
- funeral wreath;
- book about surviving a spouse's death;
- pornographic mail sent to neighbors in David Steiner's name;
- Craigslist posts;
- surveillance;
- attempted GPS placement.

The most important storytelling choice is restraint: one or two objects are enough to communicate escalation. The episode should spend more time on **why a Fortune 500 security apparatus was doing this** than on a catalogue of shock props.

### Phase 4 — police contact and cover-up
The Steiners contacted Natick police. NPD began investigating August 16.

eBay's own internal investigation started August 22 after police contact.

The admitted/established record includes:
- false statements;
- deletion of digital evidence;
- falsification of records;
- efforts to divert investigators.

By late August/September, eBay placed key security personnel on leave and terminated implicated personnel/contract arrangements.

### Phase 5 — individual criminal accountability
Federal prosecutors charged seven former eBay employees/contractors.

All seven ultimately pleaded guilty.

Most narratively important consequences:
- Baugh: 57 months imprisonment + supervised release + fine.
- Harville: 24 months imprisonment + supervised release + fine.
- Popp: one year and one day imprisonment.
- Cooke: 18 months imprisonment plus home detention.
- Stockwell / Zea: probation with home-confinement terms.
- Gilbert: final defendant sentenced in July 2024.

No script should say "seven executives were convicted." They were employees/contractors with differing titles and responsibility levels.

## Corporate criminal mechanism

### 2024 Information
The U.S. Attorney filed six felony counts against eBay:
- two interstate-travel stalking counts;
- two electronic-communications stalking counts;
- witness tampering;
- destruction/alteration/fabrication of records in a federal investigation.

### DPA
This is the key legal/business mechanism for the channel.

Under the DPA:
- prosecution of the corporate counts is deferred;
- eBay admitted a detailed factual recitation;
- eBay agreed to pay $3M, described by DOJ as the statutory maximum fine for the six offenses;
- eBay accepted an independent compliance monitor for three years;
- eBay agreed to compliance enhancements and continuing cooperation;
- successful completion is designed to lead to dismissal of the Information.

Important:
**Do not call eBay "convicted."**
The correct status is corporate charge + deferred prosecution agreement + admission + penalty.

## Civil case

Case:
**Steiner et al. v. eBay Inc. et al., 1:21-cv-11181**

Filed:
July 21, 2021.

The civil complaint made broad allegations including corporate/executive responsibility. Those allegations cannot simply be repeated as fact.

### Why the August 2025 order matters
The summary-judgment order is our best bridge between raw allegations and trial-ready evidence.

The court stated that, drawing reasonable inferences for the Steiners, it treated its background facts as undisputed. It then:
- granted some claims/parts to defendants;
- denied other summary-judgment requests;
- allowed major theories against eBay and executive defendants to continue;
- granted PFC summary judgment.

For Wenig:
The court noted Baugh clarified that Wenig never communicated directly with him regarding the Steiners. But the court held Wenig's messages to Wymer could, viewed in plaintiffs' favor, support a jury finding of inducement/substantial contribution. That is **not** the same as the court finding Wenig liable.

For Wymer:
The court described the communication record as extensive and held a reasonable jury could find inducement/encouragement/authorization. Again: triable issue, not final liability.

For Jones:
The court found enough evidence for relevant theories to continue, while also stating no evidence showed she had actual knowledge of the exact tortious conduct before the police report.

For eBay:
Important vicarious/ratification and damages theories remained in the case, while several discrete claims were resolved in eBay's favor.

This nuance must survive into script.

## The settlement chronology — do not compress

### February 25, 2026
A settlement in principle was reported days before trial. Terms were not disclosed and the court conditionally dismissed the case while the parties attempted to consummate it.

### June 2026
The deal failed to finalize. The case was reopened, and a January 4, 2027 trial date was set.

### July 27–28, 2026
A new final resolution was publicly announced.

Announced package:
- $48.7M compensation to the Steiners;
- $7M charitable commitments;
- total package: $55.7M.

Announced compensation allocation:
- eBay: $46.15M;
- Devin Wenig: $2M;
- Wendy Jones: $500K;
- Steve Wymer: $50K.

Charitable commitments:
- eBay: $6M to nonprofits;
- Wenig: $1M to a First Amendment-focused charity in Ina Steiner's name.

The eBay public statement confirms the agreement but does not itself list the dollar breakdown.

Therefore:
- **"$55.7M settlement package"** is safe with S014 + S017 + S018.
- **"eBay paid $55.7M"** is false/misleading.
- **"eBay's share of direct compensation was $46.15M"** is supported by the announced terms.
- **"$56M judgment"** is false; there was no judgment awarding that amount.

## Competing / opposing positions

### eBay position
eBay says:
- the former employees' conduct was wrong and reprehensible;
- it takes responsibility for former-employee misconduct under the DPA;
- the company cooperated extensively with prosecutors;
- leadership, controls, policies and training have changed;
- in 2026 it acknowledged unprofessional tone, to different degrees, in communications by Wenig, Wymer and Jones.

### Important limitation on executive culpability
eBay's internal-investigation position, as reported by the Boston Globe from a 2021 presentation, was that it found no evidence Wenig or Wymer directed or knew criminal acts would follow.

The 2025 court order does **not** make a final liability finding against Wenig, Wymer or Jones. It decides what could go to a jury.

The settlement prevented that jury determination.
