# VIDEO 001 — 02 Case / Story Map

Status: **PASS**

Story-map lock date: 2026-09-20

## Story thesis

This is not primarily a story about bizarre packages.

It is a story about **how criticism of a giant company moved through an executive chain into a corporate security function, escalated into criminal conduct and obstruction, and eventually produced personal convictions, a corporate deferred-prosecution agreement, years of civil litigation and a $55.7 million final settlement package.**

The central WHAT IT COST idea:

**A reputation problem became a security operation.  
The security operation became a criminal problem.  
The criminal problem became a corporate-liability problem.  
And the cost kept rising long after the harassment stopped.**

## Viewer promise

By the end, the viewer should understand:

- why two small publishers became a serious internal concern at eBay;
- how internal executive pressure moved toward the security organization;
- how the operation escalated from intimidation into surveillance and obstruction;
- what was actually admitted or established versus what remained disputed;
- why guilty pleas by individual employees did not end eBay's exposure;
- what a deferred prosecution agreement meant for eBay;
- why the civil case nearly went to trial;
- why the first 2026 settlement did not end the case;
- what the final $55.7M package actually consisted of.

## Narrative order

The documentary should **not** begin in 1999 and walk forward linearly.

The recommended reveal order is designed for a 17–20 minute first cut.

### Beat 0 — Cold open: the absurd object and the giant company
Target: ~0:00–0:45

Open on one concrete disturbing delivery, preferably the cockroaches or bloody pig mask.

Immediate inversion:

**This did not come from an anonymous internet troll.  
It came out of the security organization of eBay.**

Then flash forward only far enough to establish consequence:

- seven prosecuted former employees/contractors ultimately pleaded guilty;
- eBay later entered a federal DPA;
- in 2026 the civil case ended in a $55.7M settlement package.

Do **not** explain the settlement allocation yet.

Purpose:
- high curiosity;
- recognizable brand;
- immediate scale jump;
- promise that the story has a measurable ending.

Claim refs:
C010, C016, C019, C028.

### Beat 1 — Why would eBay care about two publishers?
Target: ~0:45–2:15

Introduce Ina and David Steiner and EcommerceBytes.

Key contrast:
- two small independent publishers;
- one of the world's largest online marketplaces;
- the conflict begins with coverage and reader commentary, not a conventional business rival.

The dramatic question becomes:

**How does criticism of a company become a security problem?**

Claim refs:
C001, C003.

### Beat 2 — The internal temperature rises
Target: ~2:15–4:00

Reveal internal executive messages in escalating order:

2. "take her down."

Target: ~12:45–15:00

## Main reveal

The biggest reveal is not the cockroaches.

It is that the public record shows an **organizational path** from executive hostility to security activity, while the legal record simultaneously requires discipline about exactly how far responsibility can be attributed to each executive.

That tension gives the episode both drama and credibility.

## Legal exposition needed

Keep legal explanation under roughly 90 seconds total across the episode.

### 1. Guilty plea
One sentence:
A guilty plea establishes criminal guilt without a trial.

### 2. Deferred prosecution agreement
Two to three sentences:
Charges are filed, prosecution is deferred subject to conditions, and successful completion is intended to produce dismissal.

### 3. Summary judgment
Two sentences:
Before trial, a judge can remove claims when no reasonable jury could find for the other side. When summary judgment is denied, it does not mean the defendant lost; it means the issue can still go to a jury.

### 4. Settlement
One sentence:
Settlement resolves the dispute without a verdict and does not automatically prove every allegation.

Do not teach doctrine beyond what changes the story.

## Story risks

### Risk 1 — sensationalism
If the episode over-focuses on insects/masks, it becomes recycled true crime.

Fix:
Use one or two objects as an entry point and move immediately to organizational mechanism.

### Risk 2 — executive overstatement
Internal messages are visually powerful and easy to over-interpret.

Fix:
Pair the strongest messages with the legal counterweight:
documented communication ≠ criminal conviction or final civil liability.

### Risk 3 — money confusion
Multiple dollar amounts create packaging risk.

Fix:
Use separate on-screen buckets:
- CRIMINAL DPA: $3M + monitor
- CIVIL SETTLEMENT PACKAGE: $55.7M

### Risk 4 — too many names
The record has many participants.

Fix:
Three-layer visual hierarchy:
executives → Baugh/security leadership → supporting team.
