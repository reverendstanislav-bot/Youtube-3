## Material review findings

### F01 — S02 categorical “not a record label”

V1 says:

**“DistroKid is not Spotify, and it is not a record label.”**

The first half is fine. The second half is too categorical.

DistroKid primarily markets itself as a music distribution service, but its current site also says it has services for labels, and the complaint itself notes that DistroKid offers some label-like services.

**Required Stage 05 fix:**
Use narrower role language, for example:

**“DistroKid is not Spotify. It primarily markets itself as the distribution pipe in between.”**

Do not make a categorical “not a record label” claim.

### F02 — S02 independence/generalization language

V1 says a creator “no longer needs a traditional label relationship just to get a release delivered around the world.”

The concept is understandable but broader than necessary.

**Required Stage 05 fix:**
Tie the point to DistroKid's own service proposition:

**“Services like DistroKid let independent artists deliver releases to major streaming platforms without relying on traditional distribution infrastructure.”**

This keeps the business-mechanism point without making a universal industry claim.

### F04 — S08 $150,000 statutory-damages standard

V1 is directionally correct and does not invent a multiplied total.

For maximum legal precision, the statutory condition should be stated explicitly.

17 U.S.C. § 504(c)(2) provides that where the copyright owner proves and the court finds willful infringement, the court **may in its discretion** increase statutory damages to not more than $150,000 per work.

UMG's complaint requests that maximum.

**Required Stage 05 fix:**
Prefer:

**“UMG asks for the statutory maximum. Under the Copyright Act, a court may go up to $150,000 per work if willfulness is proved and the court finds it, but no such finding or award exists here.”**

Keep the no-multiplication rule.
