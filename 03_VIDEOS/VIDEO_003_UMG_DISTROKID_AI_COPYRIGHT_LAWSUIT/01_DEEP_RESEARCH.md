## What UMG alleges
The complaint alleges deceptive trade practices and several copyright theories. It expressly says the lawsuit is **not about AI-generated music when clearly disclosed as such**.

UMG alleges distribution of altered/remixed copies, copied cover art, instrumentals with substituted vocals, unlicensed samples and compilations incorporating protected recordings.

Scale allegations:
- "Lofi Chill" — **4,562 tracks in twelve months**;
- Exhibits A/B — **1,000 examples**;
- plaintiffs' investigation — **nearly 2,000** allegedly infringing DistroKid-distributed recordings.

All remain plaintiffs' assertions.

UMG also alleges rights-management systems notified DistroKid of conflicts and that, in some instances, distribution continued elsewhere after acknowledgement. This is a central hidden mechanism but remains unadjudicated.

## ISRC / metadata
UMG alleges "ISRC theft." Official ISRC guidance says ISRC uniquely and persistently identifies a sound or music-video recording. This explains why a small metadata identifier can matter downstream for rights and revenue routing.

## What DistroKid says
DistroKid's public response says it supports independent artists, takes copyright protection/fraud prevention seriously, continuously invests in protections, **strongly disagrees with UMG's allegations**, and intends to defend itself vigorously.

Its current AI-upload policy says AI-created music is permitted only if the uploader owns the rights, avoids unauthorized impersonation, avoids mass-generated spam and does not infringe. Treat this as stated policy, not proof of universal compliance.

## Money / business stakes
The complaint does not state one fixed case value. It seeks statutory damages up to the applicable maximum per infringed work, or actual damages/profits, plus injunctions, impoundment/destruction, fees/costs and other relief.

UMG alleges willfulness and requests **$150,000 per work** where applicable. That is a requested statutory ceiling conditioned on legal findings — **not an award, guaranteed recovery or single total case value**.

Operational stakes may be more important: account termination, takedowns, upload verification, post-notice handling, platform trust and high-volume distribution economics.
