## Hidden mechanism

The hidden mechanism is **distribution responsibility after scale and notice**:

**creator/upload account  
→ audio + metadata  
→ distributor  
→ streaming platforms  
→ rights-management conflict / notice  
→ decision to remove, investigate or continue distribution  
→ potential legal exposure**

The film becomes weaker and less accurate if framed as:
"AI music is illegal."

It becomes stronger as:
**"The lawsuit is testing what happens when the middleman that makes distribution frictionless is accused of letting disputed material keep moving."**

## Main reveal

The main reveal is that **the case is really about the middle layer**.

The most important narrative move is:

**This is not just a dispute over who made a questionable track.  
It is a dispute over what the distributor allegedly knew, what metadata and rights-management systems signaled, and what happened after those signals appeared.**

That connects creator economy, AI, copyright, platform systems and business incentives without pretending the court has resolved any of them.

## Legal exposition needed

Keep legal explanation under roughly 90–120 seconds total across the film.

### 1. Allegation vs finding
One sentence early:
A complaint states what the plaintiff alleges; it does not establish liability.

### 2. Copyright statutory damages
Two to three sentences:
Copyright law can allow statutory damages within a range, with a higher ceiling for qualifying willful infringement. UMG asks for the maximum where applicable, but no award exists and no fixed aggregate total is stated.

### 3. Notice / knowledge
Explain conceptually only:
UMG's theory becomes more serious if it can prove DistroKid knew of specific rights conflicts and still engaged in legally actionable distribution conduct. Do not prejudge whether the facts or legal standard are satisfied.

### 4. ISRC
One sentence:
An ISRC is a persistent identifier for a specific sound or music-video recording; metadata disputes do not themselves establish infringement.

No broad copyright-law lecture.
