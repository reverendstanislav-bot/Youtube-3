# VIDEO 002 — R8 Approved Frame Style Lock

Status: **OWNER-APPROVED VISUAL DIRECTION**
Date: 2026-09-27

This file codifies the approved look demonstrated by the accepted reference frames: large informative source material, a bold but controlled headline, warm paper, deep charcoal, restrained red editorial marks, and cinematic evidence-focused lighting.

## Non-negotiable output

Every generated image is a **finished 16:9 frame**, immediately usable in the edit.

Every frame must contain:
- the exact locked headline;
- the authentic bound source material required by the beat;
- the scene-specific information the narration is discussing;
- final composition and lighting;
- a clean lower **15%** subtitle-safe zone.

No backplates. No placeholders. No “add text later”. No empty decorative frame.

## Approved visual grammar

### 1. Information first
The source is not decoration. It is the main visual evidence.
- Documents must be large enough to read the relevant passage.
- UI must be recognizable and readable.
- Photos must be large enough to identify the location/person/event.
- When multiple sources are present, establish one clear primary source and one or two secondary sources.

### 2. Headline
- Upper-left or left-center by default.
- Off-white / ivory distressed condensed capitals.
- Usually one or two lines.
- Large enough to read instantly but never more important than the evidence.
- One short deep-red underline.
- Exact locked spelling only.
- No secondary generated copy.

### 3. Materials / light
- Deep charcoal-black textured background or desk.
- Warm aged ivory paper.
- Low-key directional key light with warm highlights.
- Fine film grain and subtle wear.
- Restrained deep-red accent only.
- Optional soft cool shadow/fill for depth.
- Premium documentary still, not YouTube clickbait.

### 4. Evidence emphasis
For legal/financial documents:
- one thin deep-red margin bracket is preferred;
- one restrained warm-yellow translucent highlight may emphasize the relevant source passage;
- never rewrite, summarize or invent source text;
- never generate a fake legal page to substitute for an authentic source.

### 5. Physical styling
Use physical props only when they add meaning:
- binder clip, pen, tag, pin, red thread, tab, folder: **maximum 1–2 supporting devices per frame**;
- do not add props merely to fill empty space;
- avoid repeated identical desk setups across consecutive shots.

### 6. Variation without breaking the brand
Rotate naturally among:
- dominant document evidence;
- layered document evidence;
- one archival photo;
- two-source photo relationship;
- authentic UI/product evidence;
- mixed evidence composition;
- restrained source-free editorial metaphor.

The channel identity comes from typography, paper, charcoal, red accents, lighting and source hierarchy — not from repeating the same desk/paper arrangement.

### 7. Hard negatives
Never:
- collage, contact sheet, storyboard or grid;
- orange collage blocks;
- fake UI;
- fake legal text;
- invented logos;
- extra people;
- cropped/warped faces;
- gavels, Lady Justice, Capitol, flags, courtroom clichés;
- piles of cash unless the script literally requires cash;
- black caption boxes;
- neon or glossy advertising aesthetics;
- random arrows, circles, giant icons;
- decorative clutter that does not communicate the beat.

## Subtitle-safe lock

Bottom **15%** of every frame stays calm: no headline, faces, key evidence, tiny text or important UI.
