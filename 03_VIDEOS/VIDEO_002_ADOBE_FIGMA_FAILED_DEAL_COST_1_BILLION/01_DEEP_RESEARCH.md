## Research conclusion

The evidence base is strong enough to advance to Stage 02 without inventing facts.

The story is materially stronger than a generic "regulators killed a tech deal" recap because the primary record exposes the hidden contractual mechanism months before the deal was signed:

**Figma negotiated protection against regulatory failure. Adobe ultimately agreed to a $1 billion reverse termination fee. When the companies later concluded there was no clear path to required approvals, they mutually ended the acquisition — and the contractual protection became a real $1 billion cash payment.**

That is the correct WHAT IT COST spine.

## Parties / entities and roles

### Adobe Inc.
Public software company. Signed the September 2022 merger agreement as buyer/parent. Proposed consideration was approximately $20B, roughly half cash and half Adobe stock. Ultimately paid Figma $1B after mutual termination.

### Figma Inc.
Web-first collaborative product-design company. Was privately held during the transaction. Negotiation record shows Figma sought a reverse termination fee linked to regulatory-closing risk. Remained independent after the deal ended and completed an IPO in 2025.

### UK Competition and Markets Authority
Reviewed the anticipated transaction. Opened Phase 1 in May 2023, referred the deal to Phase 2 in July, published provisional findings and possible remedies in November, then cancelled the inquiry after abandonment.

### European Commission
Case M.11033. Opened an in-depth investigation in August 2023 and adopted a Statement of Objections in November. The transaction was abandoned before a formal Commission decision on the merits.

### U.S. Department of Justice — Antitrust Division
Conducted an investigation. After abandonment, AAG Jonathan Kanter publicly welcomed the decision. Stage 01 found no public DOJ complaint or merger-blocking court judgment.

## Canonical chronology

### Phase 1 — the price gets negotiated before the risk does
Adobe/Figma contacts went back years, but serious 2022 acquisition talks restarted in April.

Adobe's June 19 proposal:
- $20B acquisition price;
- about half cash / half stock;
- no termination fee specified.

Figma then pushed on both value and regulatory downside.

On July 5, Figma's counterproposal introduced a reverse termination fee if required antitrust approvals were not obtained.

On July 20, Adobe's proposal:
- kept the $20B acquisition price;
- kept roughly 50/50 cash and stock;
- agreed to a **$1B reverse termination fee**.

This is the first major reveal for the film: the billion-dollar consequence was not invented in December 2023. It was negotiated into the transaction in July 2022.

### Phase 2 — definitive agreement
September 15, 2022:
- definitive merger agreement signed;
- transaction publicly announced;
- closing remained subject to regulatory clearances and other conditions.

Section 8.2 of the signed contract is unusually valuable for our story because it explicitly says the termination fee is **not a penalty**, but liquidated damages.

The contract also imposed regulatory-efforts obligations, with defined limits on the remedy actions Adobe could be required to accept.

### Phase 3 — regulatory scrutiny becomes the main obstacle

#### United Kingdom
May 3, 2023:
CMA opens inquiry.

June 30:
CMA says there may be substantial-lessening-of-competition concerns.

July 13:
Phase 2 in-depth referral.

November 28:
CMA publishes provisional findings concerning:
- all-in-one product design;
- vector editing;
- raster editing.

The CMA's possible-remedies notice considered:
- prohibition;
- structural divestiture.

The remedies document says a Figma Design divestiture would be substantially similar to prohibition, with added execution risk.

Important:
These were **provisional findings and possible remedies**, not a final prohibition order.

#### European Union
June 30, 2023:
Commission receives notification.

August 7:
Commission opens in-depth investigation.

November 17:
Commission adopts Statement of Objections.

The Commission's later official merger brief explicitly says an SO communicates preliminary concerns and is not a formal final decision.

#### United States
DOJ Antitrust Division investigated the transaction.

The official DOJ statement after abandonment praises the decision and confirms a thorough investigation.

Research limit:
No public DOJ complaint or court order blocking Adobe/Figma was located in Stage 01. Do not script one.

### Phase 4 — what Adobe and Figma said back
Adobe and Figma disputed the competition theories.

Their CMA submissions argued the products/capabilities were complementary and the deal pro-competitive.

In the December remedies response, they said the CMA's remedies direction effectively pointed to prohibition or divestiture of Figma Design.

This is useful counterweight but remains advocacy, not an adjudicated fact.

### Phase 5 — the deal ends
December 17, 2023:
Adobe and Figma sign a mutual termination agreement.

December 18:
They publicly announce the abandoned transaction.

Their stated joint assessment:
there was no clear path to the required regulatory approvals from the EC and CMA.

The formal ending is therefore:
**mutual termination amid unresolved regulatory objections**, not a final regulator prohibition judgment.

The CMA closed/cancelled its Phase 2 inquiry after abandonment.

The European Commission's investigation ended without a formal merits decision.

### Phase 6 — the billion-dollar clause becomes cash
The termination agreement required Adobe to pay Figma:
**$1,000,000,000 in cash within three business days.**

The agreement describes it as liquidated damages and sole/exclusive remedy.

December 20, 2023:
- Adobe says it paid the $1B using cash on hand;
- Figma says it received $1B;
- Figma recorded it in other income;
- Adobe recorded the fee in operating expenses.

This is the cleanest payoff in the episode.
