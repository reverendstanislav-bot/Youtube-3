# VIDEO 002 — 02 Case / Story Map

Status: **PASS**

Story-map lock date: **2026-09-25**

## Story thesis

This is not primarily a story about antitrust law.

It is a story about **how a $20 billion acquisition agreement allocated regulatory risk before anyone knew whether the deal would close — and how one negotiated clause later turned a failed acquisition into a real $1 billion cash payment.**

The central WHAT IT COST idea:

**Adobe wanted Figma.  
Figma wanted protection if regulators stopped the closing.  
That protection became a $1 billion reverse termination fee.  
When the companies later concluded there was no clear path to the required approvals, the deal ended — and the clause fired.**

The strongest reveal is not that regulators had concerns.

It is that the billion-dollar consequence was written into the deal before the regulatory fight reached its peak.

## Viewer promise

By the end, the viewer should understand:

- what Adobe agreed to pay for Figma;
- why the proposed $20B purchase price and the $1B termination fee are different numbers;
- when and why the $1B reverse termination fee entered negotiations;
- what a reverse termination fee actually does;
- how UK, EU and U.S. regulatory scrutiny escalated;
- what the CMA and European Commission actually did — and did not do;
- why Adobe and Figma mutually terminated rather than waiting for final UK/EU decisions;
- why Adobe still owed Figma $1B after buying nothing;
- what happened to Figma afterward.

Viewer assumes $1B was a fine.

### Reversal 3
Regulator concerns look like the final legal outcome.
They were provisional/preliminary; the deal was abandoned before final UK/EU merits decisions.

### Reversal 4

### Reversal 5

### Reversal 6

## Legal exposition needed

Keep formal legal/procedural explanation under roughly 90 seconds total.

### 1. Reverse termination fee
Two to three sentences:
A buyer can agree in advance to pay the seller if specified closing failures occur. Here the agreement tied a $1B payment to defined regulatory/closing circumstances and called the fee liquidated damages, not a penalty.

### 2. Provisional finding
One sentence:
A provisional competition finding is a regulator's preliminary view, not the final decision.

### 3. Statement of Objections
One sentence:
It formally communicates the European Commission's preliminary concerns and gives parties a chance to respond; it is not itself a final prohibition.

### 4. Mutual termination
One sentence:
The parties ended the merger agreement by agreement before final UK/EU merits decisions.

No broader antitrust-law lecture.
