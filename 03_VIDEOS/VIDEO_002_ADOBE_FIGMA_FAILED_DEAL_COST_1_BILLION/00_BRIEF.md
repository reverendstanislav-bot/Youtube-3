# VIDEO 002 — Brief

## Working title
Adobe’s Failed Figma Deal Cost $1 Billion

## Subject / entities
- Adobe Inc.
- Figma, Inc.
- UK Competition and Markets Authority (CMA)
- European Commission
- U.S. securities filings / merger agreement record

## Pillar
DEAL_CONTRACT

## Core viewer promise
Explain how Adobe signed an approximately $20 billion agreement to acquire Figma, failed to obtain a clear path to required regulatory approvals, acquired nothing, and still paid Figma a contractual $1 billion termination fee.

The film is about **deal structure + regulatory risk + consequence**, not an antitrust law lecture.

## Hidden problem / mechanism
The key hidden mechanism is the **$1 billion reverse termination fee** built into the merger agreement. The payment was a contractual allocation of regulatory-closing risk, not a government fine or penalty.

## Money / stakes
- proposed acquisition value: approximately **$20 billion**
- Adobe termination payment to Figma: **$1.0 billion**
- payment date: **December 20, 2023**
- Figma later remained independent and completed an IPO in **August 2025**

## Conflict
Adobe and Figma wanted the transaction to close. UK and EU competition review raised material concerns. After roughly fifteen months of regulatory review, the parties concluded there was no clear path to the approvals needed to close and mutually terminated the agreement.

## Consequence
Adobe paid $1 billion without acquiring Figma. Figma remained independent, later became public, and its own SEC filings now provide a clean post-mortem of the abandoned merger and termination payment.

## Why now
The core event is evergreen, but the story became stronger after Figma's 2025 IPO and subsequent public-company filings made the post-deal outcome and accounting record easier to verify from primary sources.
