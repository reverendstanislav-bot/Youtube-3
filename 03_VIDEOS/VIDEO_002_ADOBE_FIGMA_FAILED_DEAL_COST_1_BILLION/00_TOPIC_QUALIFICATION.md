## Why it qualifies

### Recognizability
Adobe is globally recognizable and Figma is a major modern design/product platform. The deal itself was approximately $20 billion, making the business stakes immediately understandable.

### Strongest hook
**Adobe agreed to buy Figma for about $20 billion. The deal never closed. Adobe still paid Figma $1 billion.**

That consequence is documented directly in Adobe and Figma SEC filings.

### Hidden mechanism
The story turns on a contract term most viewers do not normally see: a **reverse termination fee** tied to failure to obtain required antitrust clearance under the merger agreement.

This gives the film a clear WHAT IT COST mechanism:
signed deal → regulatory risk → no clear path to clearance → termination → $1B contractual payment.

### Primary-source path
Stage 00 verified a strong primary record:
- Adobe Dec. 2023 Form 8-K / termination agreement;
- Adobe annual reports confirming the $1B payment;
- Adobe merger prospectus describing the reverse termination fee;
- Figma's own termination announcement;
- UK CMA merger inquiry and provisional findings;
- Figma SEC IPO / annual-report filings confirming receipt of the $1B fee and later independent-company history.

Primary sourceability: **5/5**.

### Current consequence
Figma remained independent and completed its IPO in August 2025. Its later public filings explicitly retain the abandoned Adobe merger and $1B termination fee in the company's financial history.

This gives the ending more consequence than a simple 2023 "deal failed" recap.

### Competition / differentiation
There are existing explainers and some later YouTube documentaries about the failed deal. The gap is not "nobody covered Adobe/Figma."

Our differentiation:
- make the reverse termination fee the narrative engine;
- use actual merger/SEC/regulatory documents;
- distinguish provisional competition concerns from a final prohibition;
- follow the money all the way through the later Figma public-company record;
- avoid unsupported "Adobe tried to kill Figma" / monopoly-motive storytelling.

Competition score is therefore good, not perfect.

## Legal / reputational guardrails

1. **$1B was a contractual termination fee, not a regulatory fine or civil penalty.**
2. Do not say a final regulator or court order "blocked" the acquisition unless Stage 01 finds a source supporting that precise procedural statement. The safer established fact is that Adobe and Figma mutually terminated after concluding there was no clear path to required regulatory approvals.
3. CMA findings cited at this point were **provisional**, not a final merits determination.
4. Do not present competition concerns as proof of Adobe's motive.
5. Do not state Adobe bought nothing "because it lost a lawsuit"; that is not the mechanism.
6. Do not claim the $1B payment caused Figma's later IPO or later valuation. Chronology is usable; causation requires evidence.
7. Keep the approximately $20B proposed purchase price distinct from the $1B termination fee.
8. Any claim about U.S. DOJ intervention must be separately sourced during Stage 01.
