## The visual language

The 15 PASS frames establish this grammar:

1. **Evidence is beautiful and dominant.** Authentic documents, UI, buildings, photos and identifiers are visually important, not tiny props.
2. **Headline + evidence form one composition.** Distressed condensed ivory headline, controlled scale, one short deep-red underline.
3. **Charcoal / warm ivory / deep red.** Deep textured charcoal environment, warm aged paper/matte surfaces, restrained deep red, occasional transparent warm-gold evidence highlight.
4. **Cinematic physical depth.** Directional warm key light, subtle cool shadow falloff, dimensional edge shadows, fine film grain, modest wear.
5. **Information first.** One beat = one instantly understandable idea. Decorative objects are subordinate.
6. **Premium, not template-like.** The film must not devolve into repeated “dark desk + random paper” shots. Change framing, scale, source type, depth, angle and light according to the beat while preserving the brand.
7. **Source fidelity is absolute.** Documents/UI/photos/logos remain the real attached source content. Never redraw or hallucinate them.

## Headline lock

- Exact locked headline only.
- Distressed condensed ivory/off-white capitals.
- Normally upper-left / left-center or natural negative space.
- One or two lines when practical.
- One short deep-red underline.
- Headline must be readable in one glance but must not crush the factual evidence.
- No secondary generated prose unless the beat prompt explicitly requires an exact verified fact label.

## Source rule

For every R10 frame:
- attach every factual source listed in that frame's prompt;
- use source pixels as the actual evidence;
- preserve source-native text, UI, faces, people, architecture, logos and proportions;
- generated environment may frame/light/mount/perspective-place the source but may not replace it;
- if the source is a document, never generate a fake page around it;
- if the source is UI, never invent controls, labels, cursors or windows;
- if the source is a photo, never alter identity/faces or invent additional people.

## Canon anchor rule

Every frame has:
- one **PRIMARY CANON ANCHOR** from the 15 PASS set;
- one **SECONDARY CANON ANCHOR**.

Canon anchors control only:
- visual hierarchy;
- typography feel;
- palette;
- material treatment;
- lighting;
- evidence emphasis;
- polish.

Canon anchors **never supply factual content** to another beat.

## Subtitle-safe rule — mandatory

The bottom **15% of the full 16:9 frame is reserved for subtitles**.

Inside that zone:
- no headline;
- no face;
- no key source wording;
- no key number/date;
- no tiny UI;
- no evidence highlight;
- no important object edge that the viewer must inspect.

Keep the lower zone calm, dark and visually simple. Never add a black caption box.

## Beauty bar

A frame can be factually correct and still be REJECT.

PASS requires:
- publication-ready art direction;
- strong hierarchy;
- premium lighting;
- tactile materials;
- clean negative space;
- no visual clutter;
- no generic stock-photo feel;
- no slideshow/PowerPoint feel;
- no cheap “legal thriller” clichés;
- no repeated template fatigue.

## Hard negatives

Never:
- fake legal body text;
- fake UI;
- invented logos;
- source lookalikes;
- extra people;
- warped/cropped faces;
- collage/contact sheet/storyboard;
- giant random arrows/circles/icons;
