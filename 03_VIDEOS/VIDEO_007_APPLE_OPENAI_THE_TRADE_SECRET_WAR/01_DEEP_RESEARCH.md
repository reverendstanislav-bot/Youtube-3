## I. The actual story in the evidence
The case is not about a verdict or a proven two-way theft. Apple (plaintiff) sued several former employees and OpenAI/io entities in July. Apple alleges former engineer Chang Liu exploited residual system access, transferred confidential hardware data and advised a colleague about avoiding scrutiny; Apple alleges long-serving ex-executive Tang Yew Tan used insider product knowledge and sought physical parts and proprietary project details in interviews. Those propositions are **plaintiff allegations in ECF1**, not established court facts.

OpenAI disputes the pleaded account, argues technical access partly persisted because of Apple's departure practices, and challenged claims through dismissal and injunction briefs. **OpenAI's public account/curated messages are party evidence offered in litigation, not neutral independent proof.**

Judge Davila on Oct13 authorized *limited expedited discovery*. He allowed targeted forensic and recruitment-interview records and limited depositions but denied early sweeping research-and-development document searches and deferred requests reliant on an overbroad definition of "Apple Originated Information." He expressly declined to weigh opposing experts' evidence of alleged deletion/spoliation in that early discovery order. This is **not a finding of misappropriation or a granted preliminary injunction**.

OpenAI's Oct14 ECF176 introduced an allegation with the opposite direction: a former Apple->OpenAI->Apple worker had, according to its still-running forensic investigation, sent over 100 files about at least one unreleased OpenAI device to personal accounts, including alleged device photos, technical designs, supplier information and manufacturing/cosmetic specifications. Some were allegedly sent early in Singapore time on June2 (last workday); OpenAI says it *understands* product plans were presented to Apple leadership. That is weaker than a judicial finding or an authenticated Apple-use trail. ECF176 requested **opportunity to investigate and potentially counterclaim, not an actual counterclaim**. Original ECF176 p2 says "Trial Date: None Set."

Reuters reported Apple disputing this and saying it found no support for the allegation in internal systems. Apple Oct14 responsive court filing is referenced in secondary reports but could not be verified as an original in this run; keep exact forensic denials attributed to spokesperson / Reuters until original is found.

## II. Evidence taxonomy / six count accuracy
- Apple's July complaint ECF1: **4 civil DTSA misappropriation counts** (Liu, Tan, OpenAI, io) and **2 breach-of-IP-agreement counts** (Liu, Tan). "Counts" are legal claims not criminal charges.
- Apple asks for preliminary/permanent injunctive orders; returns/preservation; compensatory damages, restitution/unjust-enrichment alternatives, reasonable royalty, possible exemplary relief and attorneys' fees. The *prayer is a demand*, NOT any amount awarded.
- OpenAI's Aug5 ECF59 moved to dismiss; NO dismissal grant was identified.
- Apple's pending preliminary injunction (ECF38) and expedited discovery (ECF49) are separate. ECF175 ruled on **ECF49 only**. Proceedings Oct14 and rumored Oct15 stipulations must not be conflated with ECF175.
- Original court content is primary for **the fact filings and orders exist**; adversarial assertions inside a filing remain "ALLEGED". Do not upgrade a lawyer's statement about forensics to an adjudicated truth.
- Financial hook: **no $X amount attributable to a verified award/settlement in this case as of reviewed material**. Do not repurpose reported valuation of io/OpenAI hardware as money "lost" in lawsuit.
