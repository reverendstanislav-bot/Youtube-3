## A01 — THE INVISIBLE PRODUCT

**P006**

Look at a casino machine from the player's side. You see animated symbols, features, and the possibility of a prize. What you don't see is the model determining how those events are arranged.

**P007** **[SHORT S02 — exact contiguous passage, subject to Stage04]**

Aristocrat and Light & Wonder make electronic casino games. The fight over Dragon Train wasn't simply about whether two machines showed dragons. It centered on mathematical information used to build a game: how prizes and features are configured. Some outcomes can be studied from visible play. Other internal combinations may be confidential. That distinction would matter in court.

**P008**

Imagine two games with similar bonus features. You can observe how they behave, but observing a game is not the same as possessing its internal worksheets: the design choices and calculations behind those features. That information may have taken time, expertise, and money to develop.

**P009**

And not every probability is a secret. A player, mathematician, or rival designer might infer certain patterns from what the game publicly does. The court would later make exactly that distinction.

**P010**

So to understand the lawsuit, start with the earlier games that Aristocrat says carried its valuable mathematical work.

## A02 — THE PERSON AND THE LAUNCH

For Aristocrat, the central question became whether the new team's work crossed the line from industry experience into confidential information.

**P015**

## A03 — FROM SIMILARITY TO A FILE

**P016**

In February 2024, Aristocrat sued in federal court in Nevada. Its allegations included trade-secret and other intellectual-property claims. Light & Wonder contested material allegations. At that stage, the accusations were arguments in a lawsuit, not findings made after a jury trial.

**P018**

These weren't things a casino player could inspect. They were development materials: worksheets, calculations, and choices that could reflect how a game had been built. Aristocrat argued those private details mattered far more than surface resemblance. The judge examined their relationship, not just a single coincidental cell.

It was evidence considered for a temporary injunction, not a final jury verdict.

**P020**

## A05 — THE DEFENSE

**P026**

Light & Wonder had an answer. Its developers, the company argued, had done independent work. Some final features differed, and some mathematical patterns could be derived from a game itself. That matters: a competitor can study a product without automatically stealing a trade secret.

**P027** **[SHORT S05 — exact contiguous passage, subject to Stage04]**

A federal judge partly agreed with Light & Wonder in the Dragon Train mathematics dispute. In a 2024 preliminary ruling, the court accepted that certain SCAT2 probabilities could be inferred from publicly observable features. But the judge still saw strong evidence concerning other confidential mathematical combinations. Winning that one argument did not clear the company of the broader claim.

**P028**

Think of the difference between noticing how often a visible feature appears and opening a designer's private workbook. The first might be calculated from observation. The second can reveal choices nobody outside the development team was meant to have. The law doesn't treat those things as automatically identical.

**P029**

The judge examined that distinction rather than rejecting every argument from Light & Wonder. But on important combinations of game math and documentary similarities, the preliminary analysis favored Aristocrat. That was enough to change what could happen next.

**P030**

The word preliminary matters. The court was assessing the likelihood of success and the need for interim relief, not issuing a final verdict on every trade-secret, copyright, or business claim in the lawsuit.
