## 2. The true core story, approved for Stage02 mapping
Aristocrat challenged Light & Wonder's development of **Dragon Train**, and later **Jewel of the Dragon**, alleging improper use of game-mathematics material. At a **preliminary injunction** stage in September 2024, the US judge detailed unusually specific spreadsheet/evidence similarities and rejected central parts of L&W's competing explanations, while accepting that **certain isolated probabilities could be derived** from available features. Those are interim judicial findings, not final jury findings of all allegations. L&W publicly disagreed with the preliminary ruling in September 2024.

In **January 2026**, the parties agreed to settle their core IP dispute. L&W acknowledged that **the two game families had been developed using mathematical information that Aristocrat contends proprietary/confidential** — this carefully preserves the distinction between L&W's acknowledgement of use and Aristocrat's characterization of ownership/secrecy. On the Nevada federal stipulation dated **February 26, 2026 (ECF344)**, both sides recorded that Light & Wonder **had made a USD $127.5 million payment** to Aristocrat. L&W undertook permanent commercialization cessation for both families, no further use of covered math material and destruction of covered documents. The **court vacated prior preliminary injunctions** when dismissing the US suit; the cessation promise continued as a **settlement contract obligation**, not an active preliminary order. [S001,S004,S005,S006,S007]

The **direct Aristocrat IP claims against named L&W companies in Australia** were dismissed by a court on March 11, 2026 as reported in the issuer's SEC filing. That does NOT establish that every originally named Australian individual or separate cross-claim has been disposed of. [S008,S015]

## 3. Material corrected factual errors
### A. Australia's February2025 decision — NOT an exoneration
The original Stage01 phrased this too broadly as “Australia rejected the injunction.” A full-text court judgment reproduction of **Aristocrat Technologies Australia Pty Ltd v Light & Wonder, Inc (No2) [2025] FCA64**, Burley J, NSD1410/2024, describes existing **L&W undertakings applying central US restrictions in Australia**. The Court **refused further mandatory interim orders** that Aristocrat sought, including withdrawal of existing customer licence authorizations and compelled customer communications. Court found balance-of-convenience reasons, **not** that the underlying claims were false. Court reproduction dates the judgment February 7; issuer SEC chronology says February 6. **Narration must say “in February 2025”** unless official original docket resolves exact day.
