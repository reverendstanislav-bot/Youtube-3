**Working title:** The Casino Game That Cost $127.5 Million
**Date:** 2026-10-16 | **Topic:** T010 | **Pillar:** TRADE_SECRETS_IP | **Status:** Owner-approved topic / Stage00 GO

## Viewer promise
Why did **Light & Wonder** agree to pay rival **Aristocrat** USD **$127.5 million**, then permanently stop selling two families of casino games? The competitive advantage was hidden **game mathematics**, not just a dragon-themed exterior.

## Case and business stakes
- Parties: Aristocrat Technologies/Aristocrat Leisure vs Light & Wonder/LNW Gaming and others. US civil docket **2:24-cv-00382-GMN-MDC** (Nevada), with parallel Australian litigation.
- Games: **Aristocrat Dragon Link / Lightning Link** versus L&W **Dragon Train** and **Jewel of the Dragon**; validate precise claimed math by item at Stage01.
- 2024-09-23: federal **preliminary** injunction against Dragon Train commercialization; 2025-07-17: injunction expanded to Jewel of the Dragon. Neither was a full merits jury verdict.
- 2026-01-11: public joint settlement; L&W acknowledged certain Aristocrat math information was used in developing both games; settlement compensation **$127.5M**; permanent end of commercialization of those families, covered-info non-use/destruction, and best efforts at installed-machine removal.
- 2026-02-26: federal judge dismissed US claims/counterclaims and **vacated preliminary injunctions**; court stipulation expressly confirms settlement amount was **paid**. Permanent no-commercialization commitments arise from the **agreement**, not an injunction still in force.
- L&W publicly attributed improper use to a former employee without company knowledge; present as **L&W's attributed explanation**, not criminal conviction or a finding of everything it says.
