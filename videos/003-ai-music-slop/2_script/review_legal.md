RISK HIGH — [script.md header, line 1] "He Did the Math on Fake Streams. It Cost Him $8 Million." → The working title states as fact that the $8,091,843.64 was actually incurred/lost by Smith. Per claims.csv C005/C090, this is a *consented money judgment*; whether any of it has been collected is expressly UNKNOWN, and the note for C090 flags this exact wording as blocked ("Still blocks any 'he paid' / 'he lost $8M' / 'he forfeited $8M' wording"). The script body itself is careful never to say this ("agreed to a money judgment," "the court has already written down one number"), but the title contradicts that discipline and is the single most-read/most-seen piece of text in the piece. → Safer: avoid attaching "cost him $8 million" to a completed-loss frame — e.g. "He Did the Math on Fake Streams. He Owes $8 Million." / "...A Judgment for $8 Million." / keep the brand tagline abstract ("Here's What It Cost") and let the body supply the hedge. Flag for the packaging pass too, since this working title could carry forward.

RISK MED — [S12] "Michael Lewan, who runs an industry anti-fraud group, the Music Fights Fraud Alliance, told Rolling Stone: 'If you view it as one or five or 10 percent — that is a percentage of royalties not going to artists, creators, and songwriters...' " → Per claims.csv C105 note, this is an "industry-wide" statement, "not about Smith's victims specifically." Placed immediately after the "who paid him" discussion and bridging straight into "That gap is where Smith's lawyers built their case for mercy" (which anticipates the defense's Smith-specific "no perceptible harm" argument introduced only later, in S13), the juxtaposition implies Lewan is rebutting a claim about *this* case's victims. → Safer: add a one-clause qualifier, e.g. "speaking about streaming fraud industry-wide, not Smith's case specifically" before or after the quote.

RISK MED — [S05, S07] Attribution dropped mid-paragraph between two attributed sentences, e.g. S05: "...often on family plans, because they cost less per user. {C017} **The bots ran in browser tabs on rented cloud computers, with modified macros looping the music, and VPNs hiding that it all ran from his home.** {C020} The indictment says he told his helpers..." and S07: "It was called a Master Services Agreement. **Smith would own the songs outright and supply the song and artist names. The AI company would get $2,000 a month or 15 percent of the streaming revenue, whichever was greater.**" (no attribution tag in this sentence run, two sentences after the last "According to a federal indictment"). All underlying claims are ALLEGATION-status (C020, C030). In print, one upstream "the indictment says" can cover a paragraph; in narrated audio a listener can't glance back, so a flat declarative run several sentences long risks landing as established fact. → Safer: re-anchor lightly every 2–3 sentences ("per the indictment," "prosecutors say," "the indictment alleges") rather than relying on a single tag to cover a multi-sentence run, especially any run built on ALLEGATION-status claims.
